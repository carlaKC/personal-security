# Let's Do This!

This is your checklist for the day of the party, in the order that things happen. Cover each item in your own words - nobody needs a lecture, they just need to understand why they're doing what they're doing.

## Kicking Off

* [ ] Explain what we're doing today:
  * The goal is to get everyone to a good starting state, not a perfect one. You don't have to finish everything!
  * By the end of the party, everyone should have taken a concrete step towards better security (like two-factor login on their key accounts), and know what's left to finish up at home.
* [ ] Explain who's responsible for what:
  * You're responsible for running the event, and for making sure that everyone follows the rules of engagement.
  * Everyone is responsible for their own accounts and devices, and is free to make their own decisions about them. If anyone's unsure about a step, they can skip it, or talk it through with an LLM and make their own decision.
  * But never, *ever* put passwords, recovery codes or other sensitive information into an LLM. This is the first rule of security party!
* [ ] Go through each of the rules of engagement (below), and check that everyone agrees to them.

## Rules of Engagement

1. Don't look at anyone else's screen, and keep yours to yourself while you type passwords or codes.
2. Don't touch anyone else's device. Help by pointing and explaining.
3. Never say passwords or codes out loud, or write them down for someone else.
4. Write down recovery codes in the privacy corner (if there is one), keep them face down or sealed in an envelope, and take them home.
5. Never put passwords, recovery codes or other sensitive information into an LLM.
6. No photos or screen recordings.
7. Lock your phone when you put it down.
8. Anyone can skip a step and do it at home instead, no questions asked.

These apply to you too. It'll be very tempting to grab someone's phone and "just quickly fix it" - don't.

## Threats Talk (Optional)

If you're technical, give a short talk on the threats to look out for. If not, share the resource list instead and give everyone a few minutes to read it, or send it around after the event.

* [ ] Why we're here:
  * Scams used to be expensive to run against individuals, so most people got away with weak security.
  * AI makes convincing, personalized scams cheap to produce at scale. Most attacks are automated and go after everyone, so "I'm not interesting enough to be targeted" doesn't help.
* [ ] What's changed:
  * AI-written messages are fluent and can include real details about you, so bad spelling is no longer a giveaway.
  * Voices can be cloned from a short clip, and video calls can be faked.
* [ ] What still works:
  * Scams rely on urgency, secrecy and unusual payments (gift cards, crypto, wire transfers, "safe accounts"). Watch for those, not whether something looks or sounds real.
  * Verify through a channel you already trust: hang up and call back on a saved number.
* [ ] What matters most:
  * Email: almost every account can be reset from your email, so it's the most important account to protect.
  * Phone: it receives your login codes and holds your accounts.
  * Password reuse: one breach unlocks every account that shares the password. A password manager remembers unique passwords for you.
  * Two-factor login: a stolen password isn't enough on its own. Text message codes are better than nothing, but an authenticator app or passkey is stronger.

## Today's Details

* [ ] Show everyone the privacy corner (if you have one), and the pens, paper and envelopes for recovery codes.
* [ ] Explain the password managers we're using:
  * Everyone uses the one built into their phone: Apple Passwords on iPhone, and Google Password Manager on Android. They're free, already installed and there's no extra main password to forget (which is one of the easiest ways to get locked out!).
  * They put all of your passwords in one place, so if someone gets into your Apple or Google account (or your unlocked phone), they can get to all of them. That's why we secure those first.
  * If you lose access to your Apple or Google account, you lose your passwords too, so your recovery details really matter.
  * No product is perfect, and Apple or Google could have a bug or a breach. IMO one well-protected password manager is still much safer than reusing passwords, but it's a tradeoff. You're helping set these up, not vouching for them, and you can't be responsible for their security.
* [ ] Explain how to avoid lockouts (the most likely thing to go wrong today):
  * Check your recovery details and save your recovery codes *before* turning on two-factor login.
  * Only change an important password if you can still receive codes on your phone.

## Get to Work

* [ ] Share the [party checklist](participants/02-checklist.md), and pick a set amount of time to work for (I'd suggest about 90 minutes).
* [ ] Move around the room and check on progress, without looking at screens while people type passwords or codes.
* [ ] Help by pointing and explaining, not by taking devices.
* [ ] Watch for anyone about to change a password or turn on two-factor login without their recovery details in place.
* [ ] Nudge people towards the privacy corner when they need to write down recovery codes.
* [ ] Gently call out anyone breaking the rules of engagement.
* [ ] Make sure that everyone gets at least one concrete step done.

## Wrap Up

* [ ] Go around the room and have everyone share what they got done (the wins, not the passwords!).
* [ ] Ask everyone to fill in the follow-up section of their checklist, so that they know what's left to finish at home.
* [ ] Remind everyone to take their recovery codes home, and to change any password they think someone else may have seen.
* [ ] Share [After the Party](participants/03-take-home.md) and [Protecting Your Parents](participants/04-parents.md). Older relatives are common targets for these scams, and IMO agreeing on a family safe word is the most valuable thing people can do after the party.
* [ ] If anyone's keen to run a party of their own, point them at this repo!
* [ ] Ask what worked and what didn't, and write it down.

## After the Party

* [ ] Check in with your friends a week or two later to see how their follow-ups are going.
* [ ] Update your copy of these materials with what you learned.
