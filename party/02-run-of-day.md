# Run of Day

This is your checklist for the day of the party, in order.

## Introduction Talk (~15 min)

Cover these topics in your own words. The goal is for everyone to understand why they're doing the checklist, so that the rest of the party can be spent doing it.

* [ ] Why we're here:
  * Scams used to be expensive to run against individuals, so most people got away with weak security.
  * AI makes convincing, personalized scams cheap to produce at scale. Most attacks are automated and go after everyone, so "I'm not interesting" doesn't help.
* [ ] What's changed:
  * AI-written messages are fluent and can include real details about you, so bad spelling is no longer a giveaway.
  * Voices can be cloned from a short clip, and video calls can be faked.
* [ ] What still works:
  * Scams rely on urgency, secrecy and unusual payments (gift cards, crypto, wire transfers, "safe accounts"). Watch for those, not whether something looks or sounds real.
  * Verify through a channel you already trust: hang up and call back on a saved number.
  * Don't paste passwords, ID numbers or other people's private information into AI chatbots.
* [ ] What matters most:
  * Email: almost every account can be reset from your email, so it's the most important account to protect.
  * Phone: it receives your login codes and holds your accounts.
  * Password reuse: one breach unlocks every account that shares the password. A password manager remembers unique passwords for you.
  * Two-factor login: a stolen password isn't enough on its own. Text message codes are better than nothing, but an authenticator app or passkey is stronger.
* [ ] Password managers, and their risks:
  * We're using the one built into your phone: Apple Passwords or Google Password Manager.
  * It puts all your passwords in one place. If someone gets into your Apple or Google account, or your unlocked phone, they can get to all of them. That's why we secure those first.
  * If you lose access to your Apple or Google account, you lose your passwords too, so your recovery details matter.
  * No product is perfect, and Apple or Google could have a bug or a breach. One well-protected password manager is still much safer than reused passwords, but it's a tradeoff.
  * Be clear that you're helping set these up, but you can't be responsible for the security of Apple or Google's products.
* [ ] Today's plan:
  * The goal is a good starting state: your password manager with a couple of passwords in it, and two-factor login on your key accounts.
  * Moving the rest of your passwords over is homework.
* [ ] Avoiding lockouts (the most likely thing to go wrong today):
  * Check recovery details and save recovery codes before turning on two-factor login.
  * Only change an important password if you can still receive codes on your phone.
* [ ] Rules of engagement: go through each one (below), and check that everyone agrees to them.
* [ ] Parents: mention the [parents guide](participants/04-parents.md). Older relatives are common targets for these scams, and probably aren't in the room. IMO agreeing on a family safe word is the most valuable thing people can do after the party.

## Rules of Engagement

1. Don't look at anyone else's screen, and keep yours to yourself while you type passwords or codes.
2. Don't touch anyone else's device. Help by pointing and explaining.
3. Never say passwords or codes out loud, or write them down for someone else.
4. Keep recovery codes face down, and take them home.
5. No photos or screen recordings.
6. Lock your phone when you put it down.
7. Anyone can skip a step and do it at home instead, no questions asked.

These apply to you too. It'll be very tempting to grab someone's phone and "just quickly fix it" - don't.

## Checklist Time (~90 min)

* [ ] Share the [party checklist](participants/02-checklist.md).
* [ ] Move around the room and check on progress, without looking at screens while people type passwords or codes.
* [ ] Help by pointing and explaining, not by taking devices.
* [ ] Watch for anyone about to change a password or turn on two-factor login without recovery details in place.
* [ ] Gently call out anyone breaking the rules of engagement.
* [ ] Make sure everyone gets their password manager set up with a couple of passwords in it.

## Wrap Up (~10 min)

* [ ] Ask everyone to fill in the follow-up section of their checklist.
* [ ] Remind everyone to take their recovery codes home, and to change any password they think someone else may have seen.
* [ ] Share [After the Party](participants/03-take-home.md) and [Protecting Your Parents](participants/04-parents.md).
* [ ] Ask what worked and what didn't, and write it down.

## After the Party

* [ ] Check in with your friends a week or two later about their follow-ups.
* [ ] Update these materials with what you learned (or open an issue).
