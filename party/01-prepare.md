# Preparation Guide

This guide covers how to get ready for your party, followed by a checklist of everything to do beforehand.

## Who Should I Invite?

A small group of friends who already know and trust each other. Everyone will be handling passwords and recovery codes in the same room, so this isn't one to open up to strangers or friends-of-friends you haven't met. If the group is large, ask a technical friend to help you.

## Where Should I Host?

At home, on wifi you trust. Arrange seating so that nobody is sitting directly behind someone else's screen, and make sure there are enough power outlets for everyone to charge.

## What Are the Risks?

Getting together to do this is a risk for everyone involved:
* Your friends: other people in the room may see their passwords, recovery codes or which accounts they use.
* You: if someone later gets locked out or has an account compromised, you're the person who helped. You want to be able to say with confidence that you never saw or touched anyone's secrets.

The [rules of engagement](02-run-of-day.md#rules-of-engagement) are how you keep these risks small, so read them before the party.

## Which Password Manager?

Everyone uses the password manager built into their phone: Apple Passwords on iPhone, and Google Password Manager on Android. They're free, already installed and protected by the person's Apple or Google account, so there's no separate main password to forget.

You're helping your friends set up products made by Apple and Google, not vouching for them. You can't be responsible for the security of these products, and your friends should know that. The introduction talk covers the risks of password managers, so that everyone goes in with their eyes open.

## Checklist

* [ ] Invite a small group of friends who trust each other.
* [ ] Send them [Before the Party](participants/01-prepare.md) a few days ahead.
* [ ] Set up the password manager on your own phone, so that you know the steps. If you can, have a friend with the other type of phone show you theirs (with them doing the tapping).
* [ ] Read through the [party checklist](participants/02-checklist.md).
* [ ] Read the [run of day](02-run-of-day.md), including the rules of engagement.
* [ ] If the group is large, ask a technical friend to help, and go through the rules of engagement with them.
* [ ] Check your wifi, and that there are enough power outlets (or an extension cord) for everyone.
* [ ] Plan seating so that nobody is sitting behind someone else's screen.
* [ ] Optional: pick a theme, plan food, queue up a bad movie.
