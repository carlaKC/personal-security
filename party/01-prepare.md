# Let's Get Ready!

This is everything you need to know to organize a personal security party. 

## How Does This Work?

1. Invite a small group of friends to hang out and improve their personal security together.
2. Everyone gets ready beforehand using [Before the Party](participants/01-prepare.md), and brings their phone, laptop and chargers.
3. You kick things off by explaining what you'll be doing, and going through the [rules of engagement](02-run-of-day.md#rules-of-engagement).
4. If you're technical, you can give a short talk on the threats to look out for. If not, you can just share our [resource list](participants/05-resources.md).
5. Everyone sits together for a set amount of time and works through the [party checklist](participants/02-checklist.md), focusing on the parts that are relevant to them.
6. Everyone shares what they got done (the wins, not the passwords!), and writes down their next steps to finish their basic setup.
 
## Who Should I Invite, and Where?

Invite a small group of 6-8 friends who already know and trust each other. Everyone is going to be handling passwords and recovery codes in the same room, so this isn't the time to bring along a plus one that nobody has met. Host it at home and on your own wifi.

Pro Tips:
* [ ] Have some extension cords and charging cables available, somebody's phone *will* die on you.
* [ ] Try to seat people so that nobody can look over each others shoulders.
* [ ] Think about setting up a privacy corner in a separate room or with a divider where people can write down recovery codes.
* [ ] Have pens, paper and a few envelopes on hand for recovery codes.

## What Are the Risks?

Getting a group together to sort out their security means that there's a lot of sensitive information in one room, which is a risk for everyone involved:
* Your friends: other people in the room might see their passwords, recovery codes or which accounts they use.
* You: if somebody gets locked out or has an account compromised down the line, you're the person who helped them set it up. You want to be able to say, with confidence, that you never saw or touched anyone's secrets.

The [rules of engagement](02-run-of-day.md#rules-of-engagement) are how we keep these risks small, so please read them before the party and take them seriously. Use your best judgment. If something feels off, it probably is, so go with your gut.

## Who's Responsible for What?

You're responsible for running the event: bringing people together and making sure everyone follows the rules of engagement. Everyone attending is responsible for their own accounts and devices, and is ultimately free to make their own decisions about them. If anyone is unsure about a step, they can skip it, or talk it through with an LLM and make their own decision.

But never, *ever* put passwords, recovery codes or other sensitive information into an LLM. This is the first rule of security party, and you *should* talk about it.

## Checklist

Before the event:
* [ ] Invite a small group of friends and explain the idea to them.
* [ ] Send them [Before the Party](participants/01-prepare.md) a few days ahead, so that they show up with updated phones and passwords they actually remember.
* [ ] Read through the [run of day](02-run-of-day.md), including the rules of engagement.
* [ ] Optional (if you have time): run through the [party checklist](participants/02-checklist.md) steps yourself, so that you know what to expect.
* [ ] Consider how you'll set up your space, and buy any stationery or supplies you want to provide.

On the day of the event:
* [ ] Check that your wifi is working, and that there are enough power outlets, extension cords and charging cables for everyone.
* [ ] Set up seating so that nobody is sitting behind someone else's screen.
* [ ] Put out pens, paper and envelopes for recovery codes.
