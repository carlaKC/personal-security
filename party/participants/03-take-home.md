# After the Party

This page covers what to keep in mind, how to keep your security in good shape, and what to do if something goes wrong.

## Tonight

* Put your recovery codes somewhere safe at home (still in their envelope, if you sealed them).
* If you think anyone saw a password or code during the party, change it.

## Keep in Mind

* Be suspicious of urgency. Scams pressure you to act immediately: pay, click a link, share a code or keep it secret.
* Verify through a channel you already have. Call back on a saved number, or go to the website directly instead of clicking a link.
* Voices, video, caller ID and well-written messages can all be faked.
* Legitimate companies won't ask for your password or two-factor codes.
* Never put passwords, recovery codes or other sensitive information into an LLM.

For more on all of this, take a look at the [resources](05-resources.md).

## Maintenance

* Install updates when your phone or laptop asks, or leave automatic updates on.
* When you log in to an account that isn't in your password manager yet, add it and change the password to a generated one.
* Turn on two-factor login (or a passkey) whenever an account offers it.
* Once a year, check that your recovery email and phone number are still correct, and look up your email on [Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com).

## If Things Go Badly

Act quickly and tell someone. These scams are designed to fool smart people, so there's no reason to be embarrassed.

### Someone got into one of your accounts
1. From a device you trust, change the password and sign out of all other sessions.
2. Check that your recovery email, phone number and two-factor settings haven't been changed.
3. For email, check for forwarding rules or filters that you didn't set up.
4. If you used that password anywhere else, change it there too.

### You sent money or bank details to a scammer
1. Call your bank immediately, using the number on the back of your card.
2. Report it to the police or your local fraud reporting service.

### Your phone is lost or stolen
1. From another device, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) to lock it.
2. Change your Apple or Google account password.
3. Call your carrier to block the SIM.

### Your phone suddenly has no signal
1. Someone may have moved your number to their SIM. Call your carrier from another phone immediately.
2. Check your email and bank accounts for anything unusual.
