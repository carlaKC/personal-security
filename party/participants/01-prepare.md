# Before the Party

You've been invited to a personal security party! This page covers what to bring, what to do beforehand and how we'll keep each other safe on the day. Most of the time at these events is lost to slow updates and forgotten passwords, so doing this ahead of time leaves the party for the things that matter.

## Bring

* Your phone and charger.
* Your laptop and charger, if you have one.

Your host will have pens, paper and envelopes for writing down recovery codes.

## Do Beforehand

1. Check that you can sign in to your Apple Account (or Google account on Android) and your main email. If you can't, reset the password at home.
2. Update your phone and laptop. (iPhone: Settings > General > Software Update, you'll need iOS 18 or later for the Passwords app. Android: Settings > System > Software update.)
3. Write a list of your most important accounts: email, bank, phone carrier and any social accounts you'd hate to lose. Names only, not passwords!

## Rules of Engagement

Sorting out your security in a room with other people means that passwords and recovery codes will be close at hand. We're doing this among friends who trust each other, but we'll still be careful:
1. Don't look at anyone else's screen, and keep yours to yourself while you type passwords or codes.
2. Don't touch anyone else's device. Helpers will point and explain.
3. Never say passwords or codes out loud, or write them down for someone else.
4. Write down recovery codes in the privacy corner (if there is one), keep them face down or sealed in an envelope, and take them home.
5. Never put passwords, recovery codes or other sensitive information into an LLM.
6. No photos or screen recordings.
7. Lock your phone when you put it down.
8. Anyone can skip a step and do it at home instead, no questions asked.

## Who's Responsible for What?

Your host is responsible for running the party, and for making sure that everyone sticks to the rules of engagement. You're responsible for your own accounts and devices, and you're free to make your own decisions about them. If you're unsure about a step, you can skip it, or talk it through with an LLM and make your own decision (just never, *ever* put sensitive information into one).

We'll use the password manager that's built into your phone (Apple Passwords on iPhone, Google Password Manager on Android), so there's nothing to install. Like any product it has risks, which we'll talk through at the start. Your host is helping you set it up, not vouching for it, and can't be responsible for the security of Apple or Google's products.

You don't need to be good with tech - that's the point of the party!
