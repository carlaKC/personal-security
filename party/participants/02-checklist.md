# Party Checklist

This checklist gets you to a good starting state, not a perfect one. Work through it in order, since each section builds on the one before, and ask for help if you get stuck. You don't have to finish everything! The goal is to take at least one concrete step today, and know what's left to finish at home.

Rules of engagement:
* Don't look at other people's screens, or touch other people's devices.
* Never say passwords or codes out loud.
* Write down recovery codes in the privacy corner (if there is one), keep them face down or sealed in an envelope, and take them home.
* Never put passwords, recovery codes or other sensitive information into an LLM.
* Skip any step you'd rather do at home.

## 1. Your Phone

* [ ] Your phone is up to date, and automatic updates are on.
* [ ] It has a passcode of at least six digits, plus Face ID or fingerprint unlock.
* [ ] Theft protection is on (iPhone: Stolen Device Protection, Android: Theft protection).
* [ ] Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) is on.
* [ ] Backups are on (iCloud Backup or Google backup).

## 2. Your Apple or Google Account

* [ ] Your recovery email and phone number are correct.
* [ ] You have a way back in if you lose your phone: recovery codes on paper (head to the privacy corner for this), or a recovery contact on iPhone.
* [ ] Two-factor login is on.

## 3. Your Password Manager

We're using the password manager built into your phone: Apple Passwords on iPhone, or Google Password Manager on Android. It's protected by your Apple or Google account, which is why we secured that first.

* [ ] You can open your password manager, and it's syncing with your Apple or Google account.
* [ ] Your laptop can use it too (Safari or the iCloud Passwords extension for Apple, Chrome for Google), if you have one.
* [ ] You understand the tradeoff: all your passwords are in one place, so anyone who gets into your Apple or Google account, or your unlocked phone, can get to them.

## 4. Your First Passwords

* [ ] Your main email has a new, generated password saved in your password manager.
* [ ] Two-factor login is on for your email, using a passkey or authenticator app if possible (on iPhone, the Passwords app can hold your codes). Text message codes are better than nothing.
* [ ] One more important account, like your bank, is saved in your password manager.

## Optional

* [ ] Check your email address on [Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com) to see which data breaches it has appeared in. This helps you decide which passwords to change first.

## Follow Up Later

Note what you'll come back to at home:

* [ ] Move the rest of my passwords into my password manager (easiest done one at a time, as I log in to each account).
* [ ] Turn on two-factor login for: ________________________
* [ ] Ask my phone carrier for a PIN or lock that stops my number being moved to another SIM.
* [ ] Set up a safe word and scam rules with my parents (see [Protecting Your Parents](04-parents.md)).
