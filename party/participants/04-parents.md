# Protecting Your Parents From Scams

This guide helps you agree on a few rules with your parents that will protect them from scams. Older people are common targets, and AI has made scams like the "your child is in trouble" call much more convincing, since a voice can be cloned from a short clip.

The conversation takes about 15 minutes, and is best done in person.

## Agree on These

1. A family safe word: pick a random word that can't be guessed from social media (not a pet's name or birthday). Agree on it in person, and never send it by text or online. If anyone calls claiming to be family in an emergency, ask for the word first.
2. Hang up and call back: if a call is about money, an emergency or an account problem, hang up and call back on a number you already have (a saved contact, or the number on the back of a bank card). This applies to calls from "family", banks, police and tech support.
3. Check before paying: before sending money that someone is urgently asking for, call you (or another trusted person) first. Scams rely on panic and secrecy, and a second person breaks both.
4. Always a scam: paying with gift cards or crypto, moving money to a "safe account", a stranger asking to control your computer, or anyone asking for a code that was just sent to your phone.
5. No shame: if they do get caught out, they tell you immediately. The sooner the bank knows, the better the chance of recovering the money.

## Common Scams

- "Hi Mom/Mum, I lost my phone, this is my new number" messages.
- A family emergency call (an arrest, an accident, stuck abroad), possibly in a cloned voice.
- The bank's "fraud department" calling to help move your money somewhere safe.
- Pop-ups or calls saying your computer has a virus.
- Texts about a missed delivery or an unpaid fine, with a link.
- Investment opportunities or online romances that eventually need money.

## While You're There

If they're open to it, work through the party checklist with them as well. Let them type their own passwords, so that their secrets stay theirs. Then write down your number and their bank's number somewhere they can easily find it.
