# Resources

These are short, approachable reads (and a few videos) from people who know what they're talking about: government cybersecurity agencies, consumer protection agencies and digital rights groups. Most of them take less than five minutes. A few are written for a specific country, but the advice applies anywhere.

## Scams and AI

* [Criminals Use Generative AI to Facilitate Financial Fraud](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203) (FBI): how scammers use AI-written messages, fake images and video, and cloned voices, and what you can do about it (including agreeing on a family safe word).
* [Family Emergency Scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/scams/family-emergency-scams) (US Federal Trade Commission): what the "your family member is in trouble" call looks like, and what to do if you get one.
* [How to Recognize and Avoid Phishing Scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-avoid-phishing-scams) (US Federal Trade Commission): the warning signs of a scam email or text, and what to do with one.
* [Why You Need a Family Safe Word](https://www.welivesecurity.com/en/cybersecurity/safe-word-what-why-need-one/) (ESET): how a safe word works, and how to pick a good one.

## The Basics

* [Secure Our World videos](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world-videos) (US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency): short videos on the four basics - strong passwords and a password manager, two-factor login, updates and spotting phishing.
* [Top Tips for Staying Secure Online](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/top-tips-for-staying-secure-online) (UK National Cyber Security Centre): a handful of practical tips, starting with protecting your email.
* [How to: Enable Two-factor Authentication](https://ssd.eff.org/module/how-enable-two-factor-authentication) (Electronic Frontier Foundation): what two-factor login is, and the different ways to do it.

## Your Password Manager

* [Apple Passwords](https://support.apple.com/120758) (Apple): how to use the Passwords app on your iPhone and other Apple devices.
* [Google Password Manager](https://passwords.google.com) (Google): open this in a browser to see and manage the passwords saved to your Google account.

## Check Yourself

* [Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com): check whether your email address has appeared in a data breach, so that you know which passwords to change first.
